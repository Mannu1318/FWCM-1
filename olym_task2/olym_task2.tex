{\LARGE \textbf{Twenty-third International Olympiad,1982}}

\vspace{0.1cm}

\noindent
\text{1982/1.}
The function $f(n)$ is defined for all positive integers $n$
and takes on non-negative integer values. Also, for all $m,n$

$$
f(m+n)-f(m)-f(n)=0 \text{ or } 1
$$

$$
f(2)=0,\; f(3)>0,\; \text{and}\; f(9999)=3333.
$$

\noindent
Determine $f(1982)$.

\vspace{0.1cm}

\noindent
\text{1982/2.}
A non-isosceles triangle $A_1A_2A_3$ is given with sides
$a_1,a_2,a_3$ ($a_i$ is the side opposite $A_i$).
For all $i=1,2,3$, $M_i$ is the midpoint of side $a_i$,
and $T_i$ is the point where the incircle touches side $a_i$.
Denote by $S_i$ the reflection of $T_i$ in the interior bisector
of angle $A_i$. Prove that the lines $M_1S_1$, $M_2S_2$,
and $M_3S_3$ are concurrent.

\vspace{0.1cm}

\noindent
\text{1982/3.}
Consider the infinite sequences $\{x_n\}$ of positive real
numbers with the following properties:

$$
x_0=1,\; \text{and for all}\; i\ge0,\; x_{i+1}\le x_i.
$$

\noindent

(a) Prove that for every such sequence, there is an $n\ge1$
such that

$$
\frac{x_0^2}{x_1}+\frac{x_1^2}{x_2}+\cdots+
\frac{x_{n-1}^2}{x_n}\ge3.999.
$$

\noindent
(b) Find such a sequence for which

$$
\frac{x_0^2}{x_1}+\frac{x_1^2}{x_2}+\cdots+
\frac{x_{n-1}^2}{x_n}<4.
$$

\vspace{0.3cm}

\noindent
\text{1982/4.}
Prove that if $n$ is a positive integer such that the equation

$$
x^3-3xy^2+y^3=n
$$

\noindent
has a solution in integers $(x,y)$, then it has at least three
such solutions. Show that the equation has no solutions in
integers when $n=2891$.

\vspace{0.1cm}

\noindent
\text{1982/5.}
The diagonals $AC$ and $CE$ of the regular hexagon
$ABCDEF$ are divided by the inner points $M$ and $N$,
respectively, so that

$$
\frac{AM}{AC}=\frac{CN}{CE}=r.
$$

\noindent
Determine $r$ if $B,M,$ and $N$ are collinear.

\vspace{1.5cm}

\noindent
\text{1982/6.}
Let $S$ be a square with sides of length $100$, and let $L$
be a path within $S$ which does not meet itself and which is
composed of line segments $A_0A_1,A_1A_2,\ldots,A_{n-1}A_n$
with $A_0\ne A_n$. Suppose that for every point $P$ of the
boundary of $S$ there is a point of $L$ at a distance from $P$
not greater than $1/2$. Prove that there are two points $X$
and $Y$ in $L$ such that the distance between $X$ and $Y$
is not greater than $1$, and the length of that part of $L$
which lies between $X$ and $Y$ is not smaller than $198$.
